In a groundbreaking move that has left the nation in stitches, the Department of Transportation announced today that it will begin issuing "emergency" parking permits solely for people who “really need to run into the store for just five minutes." The permits, which are valid for any parking space in the country, will come with a bold sticker reading “I Swear I’ll Be Back Before the Meter Runs Out” and will be accompanied by a short instructional manual on how to dodge traffic enforcement officers with the grace and agility of a ballet dancer.  Transportation Secretary Chuck Wheeler, known for his lighthearted approach to bureaucratic red tape, defended the initiative at a press conference surrounded by a group of people sporting their newly minted permits. “Look, we all know that nothing is more pressing than the urgent need for a bag of chips or a last-minute pint of ice cream. Who hasn’t been there?” he said, gesturing dramatically as if to emphasize the gravity of the situation. “This is a way for Americans to reclaim their time. We are saying that the existential dread of a snack emergency is just as serious as the threat of a speeding ticket.”  Critics of the new policy have argued that it encourages irresponsible parking behavior, but the Department of Transportation has countered with compelling evidence: a survey conducted by a team of highly motivated college students showed that 87% of respondents feel that the world might just end if they don't grab their favorite snacks immediately. “When you’re on a mission for nachos, the clock is ticking,” said a spokesman, who wished to remain anonymous, presumably out of concern for his own snack-related credibility.  To celebrate the launch of these revolutionary permits, the Department has also announced plans for a new traffic safety campaign featuring a cartoonish superhero known as “Captain Convenience.” Captain Convenience will not only promote responsible shopping but will also give tips on how to make it back to your car before the dreaded ticket arrives.   Local businesses are reportedly thrilled with the initiative. “We’ve already seen a spike in sales of convenience items,” said Gina, the owner of a nearby corner store. “Before this, people would just park in the lot for 20 minutes pretending to look at the store window. Now, they’ll park, dash in, and actually buy something! It’s a win-win situation, as long as they remember to use their permits, of course.”   As the nation braces itself for what is sure to be an influx of “just five-minute” parking violations, it is clear that the battle between snack cravings and parking enforcement has officially entered a new, and hilariously absurd, chapter.

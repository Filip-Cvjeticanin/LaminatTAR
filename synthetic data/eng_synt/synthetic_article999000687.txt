For example, if you’re faced with a looming deadline, ProcrastinAI will suggest strategies such as “reorganizing your sock drawer by color,” or “researching the history of the stapler.”   One beta tester, an overwhelmed college student named “Slackathon,” reported, “I started with a plan to write a 10-page paper and ended up spending three hours learning how to make the perfect avocado toast. I mean, who knew there were *so* many tutorials on that?”  Critics of ProcrastinAI argue that it may encourage a generation of over-caffeinated individuals glued to their screens, but supporters argue that it’s simply a natural evolution of productivity in the digital age. “It’s like having a personal trainer for procrastination,” claimed one enthusiastic user. “I never knew I could feel so accomplished while doing absolutely nothing!”  In addition to its distraction suggestions, ProcrastinAI has a built-in feature that can generate elaborate excuses for any situation. The launch of ProcrastinAI has even sparked a new trend in social media with the hashtag #ProcrastinAIChallenge, where users compete to see who can come up with the most creative excuse for failing to meet a deadline. Early submissions include things like “I got lost in a labyrinth of Netflix options” and “My brain was temporarily out of service.”  As the world braces for the impending wave of procrastination on an epic scale, one thing is clear: If we’re going to do nothing, we might as well do it efficiently.

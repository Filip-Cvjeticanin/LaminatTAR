In a groundbreaking move that has shocked the nation, the United States Congress has officially designated the third Thursday of every month as "National Procrastination Day." This decision came after a lengthy debate that was put off for nearly a year because, well, lawmakers were too busy binge-watching their favorite shows on Netflix and scrolling through social media.  Senator Bob Delay, the visionary behind the bill, stated during a press conference, “We realized that everyone is always putting things off—whether it’s filing taxes, cleaning out the garage, or simply responding to that email from your Aunt Marge about the family reunion. So why not celebrate it? It’s the ultimate American pastime!”   Critics were quick to point out the absurdity of designating a day to procrastinate, arguing that it only legitimizes America’s chronic delay in getting things done. “It’s like giving a medal for showing up late to the race,” remarked political analyst Sue Spots. “Next, they’ll create a National ‘I’ll Get to That Later’ Month, and I don’t think my heart can take that level of apathy.”  However, supporters of the bill argue that it will create a national holiday that resonates with the everyday American, allowing people to finally embrace their inner sloth. “Procrastination is an art form,” said life coach and self-proclaimed procrastination expert, Tim Tardy. “Some people can really tap into their potential for avoidance. I mean, I’ve been working on my TED Talk about procrastination for over three years now—and I haven’t even started!”  In anticipation of National Procrastination Day, companies across the nation are already rebranding their products to suit the holiday. Fast-food chains are introducing limited-time “Just Wait” burger specials, while coffee shops are offering “I’ll Have That Later” drinks—extra whipped cream, of course. Social media has bloomed with hashtags like #ProcrastinationNation and #LaterIsBetter, with memes of sloths and couch potatoes taking over feeds.  Some skeptics worry that this holiday may contribute to a culture of laziness. “What’s next?” asked one concerned citizen. “A national holiday for binge-watching? Oh wait…”   As the clock ticks down to the inaugural National Procrastination Day, citizens across the nation are encouraged to mark their calendars…well, eventually. After all, what’s the rush?

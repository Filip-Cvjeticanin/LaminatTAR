In a groundbreaking revelation that is shaking the very foundations of both science and our collective sanity, researchers at the University of Applied Absurdity have announced that they have successfully taught a group of goldfish to drive miniature cars. This monumental achievement, they claim, could change the face of pet ownership forever.  Lead researcher Dr. Splashy Waters stated in a press conference, “For too long, humans have underestimated the cognitive abilities of our aquatic friends. We felt it was time to give fish the opportunity to experience what it means to be truly ‘fin-tastic’ behind the wheel.” The team utilized a special training program that combined positive reinforcement, a carefully curated playlist of bubble-themed music, and, of course, a steady supply of fish flakes as a reward for their driving skills.  The prototype of this ambitious project involved a miniature racetrack composed of brightly colored coral reefs, complete with tiny traffic lights and a drive-thru taco stand (because who wouldn’t want tacos after a long day of driving?). The fish were fitted with tiny helmets to ensure their safety, although the team did acknowledge that the helmets were mostly for show since, well, they’re fish.  Early trials have already yielded some impressive results; one particularly ambitious goldfish named “Turbo” reportedly managed to complete a full lap in just under 15 minutes, which, according to Dr. Waters, “is a new world record for any fish that is not a dolphin in disguise.” Observers were left awestruck as Turbo navigated the track, although there were complaints from bystanders about his apparent disregard for speed limits and the occasional erratic swerving that seemed to imply he might have been driving under the influence of too many algae snacks.  PETA has expressed concern over the ethics of teaching fish to drive, arguing that fish are naturally terrible at parallel parking and tend to get easily distracted by shiny objects. In response, Dr. Waters reassured the public that all fish involved in the study were volunteers and would receive a “fishy” driver’s license upon completion of the course.  As the world reels from this astonishing development, local pet owners are already speculating about the implications on their daily lives. Imagine pulling up next to a goldfish at a red light or hearing the gentle sounds of a fish horn echoing down the street. One excited hamster owner was overheard saying, “If goldfish can drive, then my hamster could totally run a taco stand. I’m just saying!”  In a spectacular twist, the team is now considering expanding their program to teach other pets to drive as well, with cats being the next logical candidate. However, experts warn that training felines could lead to potential chaos, given their notorious disdain for following rules—especially the one about not driving on the kitchen counter.

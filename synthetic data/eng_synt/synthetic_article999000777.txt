In a groundbreaking study released by the Institute for the Advancement of Mundane Life, researchers have discovered that the average American spends a staggering 90% of their waking hours engaging in utterly pointless activities. This revelation has sent shockwaves through the nation, prompting calls for immediate action and, ironically, a meeting to discuss future meetings.  The study revealed that the top three most common pointless activities include scrolling through social media, arguing with strangers about politics, and attempting to find the perfect avocado. "It's alarming," said lead researcher Dr. Phil McCracken. "We found that even when people are ‘working from home,’ they're often just perfecting their TikTok dance moves while neglecting that project due tomorrow."  The researchers also noted that the remaining 10% of time is spent contemplating life's big questions, such as "Why is my neighbor's cat staring at me?" and "Is it too late to become a professional napper?" The findings are so significant that Congress has already proposed a bill to officially declare 'Pointless Activity Awareness Month' in an effort to redirect people's energies into more productive endeavors, such as binge-watching educational documentaries on how to become a cat influencer.  In light of these findings, the National Bureau of Pointless Activities has set forth a series of guidelines. They suggest replacing time-wasting activities with more enriching ones, such as taking up extreme ironing or organizing your sock drawer by color, texture, and emotional resonance. "We believe these activities can help give life a little more meaning," said Bureau spokesperson, Pat Tently. "After all, who doesn’t want to spend an afternoon asking themselves whether their red socks are happier next to their blue ones?"  Moreover, they recommend a daily regimen of ‘productive procrastination,’ which consists of doing laundry while contemplating the philosophical implications of the color beige. "Beige is the ultimate procrastination color," Tently explained. "It’s neither exciting nor boring—it’s just existing."  Critics of the study have raised eyebrows, with many suggesting that pointless activities are, in fact, a form of art. “Have you ever seen someone scrolling through Instagram? It’s like watching a performance piece about the futility of human connection,” declared local artist, Moira D’Art. “We need to celebrate these activities rather than demonize them!”  As the nation grapples with the implications of this research, one thing is clear: whether it’s engaging in heated debates with strangers online or earnestly contemplating the existential angst of a cat, Americans are determined to find meaning in the art of doing absolutely nothing.

In a shocking yet entirely predictable turn of events, local man Doug Wilson has officially declared himself the "King of Procrastination," a title he believes will earn him a spot in the hallowed halls of lazy legends, right next to the likes of John Doe, who once binge-watched an entire season of a crime drama instead of filing his taxes.  Doug, a self-proclaimed expert in delay tactics, made the announcement during a highly unproductive Zoom meeting with his colleagues, where he spent a full 45 minutes trying to remember the password to his work account — a task that had been on his to-do list for over three weeks. "It just felt like the right moment," Doug said, adjusting his pajama pants that have become his unofficial work uniform. "People need to know that procrastination isn’t just a habit; it's an art form."  Witnesses report that Doug’s crowning moment came when he finally decided to share with his coworkers how he managed to put off various responsibilities, including washing his dishes and responding to overdue emails. "I have an entire strategy," he explained, as he absentmindedly scrolled through social media for the tenth time that day. "I like to think of it as strategic prioritization: I prioritize absolutely nothing and let time figure itself out. It’s like being a time traveler but without the cool gadgets."  In a display of true procrastination prowess, Doug recently turned in a project proposal that he had intended to finish two months prior. Instead of submitting a polished document, he sent a hastily created PowerPoint filled with cat memes and a heartfelt apology, claiming that the cats were "a metaphor for delayed deadlines." His boss was so taken aback by the originality that she considered giving him a promotion — though Doug quickly declined, citing the fear of having to work harder.  Local experts in procrastination — who are always late to their own meetings — have praised Doug for his contributions to the field. "He’s a real pioneer," said Linda Ware, who has been in the procrastination business since 2008. "Most people just wait until the last minute; Doug has taken it to a whole new level by not even acknowledging the last minute exists."  As Doug continues to build his kingdom of procrastination, he plans to host seminars titled "Why Do Today What You Can Put Off Until Tomorrow?" and "The Art of the Last-Minute Panic." His motto? "Why rush when you can wait and see if it resolves itself?" In a world that demands efficiency, Doug stands firm, waving the flag of delay with a smirk and a coffee cup that still reads “World’s Okayest Procrastinator.”

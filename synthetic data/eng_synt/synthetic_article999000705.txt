In a groundbreaking scientific study released this week, researchers have confirmed that the average American spends approximately 25% of their life scrolling through social media, 50% wondering why they aren't rich yet, and the remaining 25% pretending to listen to their spouse during dinner. This extensive research has led scientists to propose a new unit of time known as the "Scrolltastic Hour," which, by their calculations, is much more relevant than the traditional hour, which is just a relic of the past used to measure how late we are to brunch.  Leading the charge in this revolutionary study is Dr. Ima Pundit, a social media analyst who has spent the last decade studying the scrolling habits of people who definitely should be reading classic literature instead. "We realized that the modern human experience is mostly about how many memes you can consume before breakfast and how rapidly you can refresh your news feed to see if anything has changed in the last two minutes," said Dr. Pundit. "It’s a full-time job, really."  The research also found that social media has drastically altered our perception of success and happiness. Apparently, the number of followers one has is now more important than the number of friends one can call in an emergency. According to the study, if you're stuck in a ditch and nobody can hear you scream, the optimal strategy is to tweet your predicament accompanied by a hashtag. “#HelpMe, #MyLifeIsInShambles” will surely attract help faster than a phone call or, heaven forbid, a face-to-face conversation.  In light of these findings, the researchers are turning their attention to developing new apps designed to enhance our scrolling experience, including “SnoozeBook,” which allows users to take naps while simultaneously scrolling through their feeds, thereby eliminating any need for actual rest. “Why waste time sleeping when you can just scroll through pictures of people sleeping?” Dr. Pundit proposed.  Meanwhile, tech giants are jumping on the bandwagon. An anonymous source from a major social media platform revealed plans to integrate a new feature that alerts users when they have been scrolling for more than five hours straight. The alert will be accompanied by a cheerful animated dancing cat that reminds users to take a break – but only if they’re willing to watch a 30-second ad first.  As society continues to evolve in the age of digital distraction, experts agree that the Scrolltastic Hour will soon replace all other measurements of time, and perhaps even human interaction. After all, if you can communicate your deepest emotions through a perfectly curated selection of emojis, who needs to talk?

In a groundbreaking study that has sent shockwaves through the scientific community, a group of researchers at the Institute of Overly Specific Studies has determined that approximately 90% of people don’t have a clue what they are doing with their lives. The study, which surveyed 3,000 participants all of whom were identified as “people” and “near people,” found that the majority of individuals responded to their life choices with a resounding, “I dunno, just winging it.”  Lead researcher Dr. Ima Guess said in a press conference, “We were astounded by our findings. When we asked participants about their career paths, 87% admitted they were either in their jobs by accident or because they thought ‘adulting’ would be a little more fun than it turned out to be.”  For instance, over half of the respondents claimed they chose their current occupation based solely on how nice the free coffee was in the break room. One respondent, who works as a rocket scientist, stated, “Honestly, I thought I’d be doing more ‘rocketing’ and less sitting in meetings about rocketing. Turns out, it’s 90% Excel spreadsheets and 10% pretending to care about paperwork.”  The study also revealed shocking statistics regarding personal relationships. A whopping 80% of participants admitted they were in their current relationships for the “free meals” and “tax benefits.” One man, who wished to remain anonymous, confessed, “I’ve been with my partner for six years, but if I’m honest, it’s mostly because they cook better than I can. Plus, I haven’t figured out how to assemble IKEA furniture alone yet.”  In a surprising twist, the researchers discovered that social media plays a crucial role in the decision-making process. The data revealed that 95% of respondents first consulted TikTok before making any significant life choices. “One minute you’re watching a dance challenge, and the next you’re convinced that becoming a professional dog groomer is your true calling,” Dr. Guess noted. “It’s fascinating how an algorithm can lead to a midlife crisis.”  In light of these findings, the Institute of Overly Specific Studies is calling for immediate action. They’ve proposed a nationwide “Life Choices Day,” where people can gather, share their life fails, and collectively agree to just "wing it" together. Participants will engage in fun activities such as “Guess My Career” and “Speed Dating for Life Decisions,” ensuring that everyone feels just as lost as they are.  As the press conference concluded, Dr. Guess added, “We may not know what we’re doing, but at least we’re doing it together—and that’s what really matters. Pass the coffee.”

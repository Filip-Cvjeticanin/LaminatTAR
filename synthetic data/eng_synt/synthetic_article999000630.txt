In a groundbreaking study released this week by the Institute for the Advancement of Pointless Studies, researchers have confirmed what many already suspected: the average adult possesses a shocking three percent more knowledge about reality TV than they do about basic world geography. The findings, which were shared exclusively with our publication, reveal that a staggering number of respondents could accurately name all the contestants from last season’s “The Real Housewives of Somewhere” while being utterly baffled by the location of Canada on a map.  Dr. Ima Jester, lead researcher of the study, expressed both amusement and exasperation, stating, “We had participants who could identify the dramatic moment when Karen threw a drink at Stephanie, but they were completely stumped when asked to locate the Pacific Ocean. It’s a cultural phenomenon that transcends logic.”  The study employed a highly scientific method known as the “What Do You Mean You Don’t Know Where Brazil Is?” approach, which involved multiple-choice questions, flashcards of reality stars, and a comprehensive section on emotional breakdowns over eliminated contestants. Out of 1,500 participants, 96% successfully identified the phrase “You’re fired!” as Donald Trump’s catchphrase but could not recall the name of a single country in South America.  Critics of the study have pointed out that perhaps the metrics used were flawed. “Who cares about geography when you can recite every line from ‘The Bachelor’?” argued self-proclaimed pop culture guru, Britney Trivia. “I think we should embrace this shift in knowledge. Who needs to know where Mali is when you can tell me the backstory of why Brad chose Jennifer over Angela?”  The researchers also noted a surprising correlation between reality TV viewership and a decrease in knowledge about important global issues. Participants who binge-watched more than three hours of reality TV daily displayed a marked inability to differentiate between a parliamentary democracy and the latest plot twist on “Survivor.”   In a final plea for sanity, Dr. Jester encouraged viewers to diversify their screen time. “We’re not saying give up on reality TV completely. Just balance it out with a documentary or two. For every episode of ‘Keeping Up with the Kardashians,’ maybe watch something like ‘Planet Earth.’ Who knows, you might learn that the Earth is actually round, and not just the shape of a giant reality TV set.”  As America continues to scroll through the latest drama on television, one thing is clear: the struggle for geographical awareness might just be the next great reality show.

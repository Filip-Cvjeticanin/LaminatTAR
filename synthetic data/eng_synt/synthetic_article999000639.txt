In a groundbreaking new study, scientists have discovered that the average adult spends approximately 90% of their life waiting—waiting for public transportation, waiting in line for coffee, or waiting for that inevitable dinner party where they’ll have to endure yet another discussion about the best way to cook quinoa. In fact, the only thing people seem to wait for with genuine enthusiasm is the weekend, which, if we’re being honest, is just another way of waiting for Monday to come back around.  The research team, comprised of professionals who have not only mastered the art of procrastination but also claim to have a Ph.D. in “Sitting in Traffic,” found that the greatest waiting moments happen during mundane errands—like standing in line at the DMV. Here, it’s believed that individuals can wait up to 30 years of their life, or at least until they forget what they originally came for and leave with a slightly less valid driver’s license.  Lead researcher Dr. Ima Waitin’ observed, “We’re wasting our potential. Why wait in line for a coffee when you could be binge-watching a series about other people waiting for coffee? In fact, we propose a new lifestyle where you can queue at home with a remote control, minimizing the actual wait time spent in line. Imagine waiting for that double-shot espresso while cozied up on your couch.”  Surprisingly, the study also revealed that waiting creates an excellent opportunity for self-reflection. Participants reported profound thoughts, such as, “Did I really need this avocado toast?” and “Is my life actually just one long line of regrets?” In response, therapists are now offering “Waiting Therapy” sessions, where clients engage in productive waiting exercises, like staring blankly at their phones while seeking profound wisdom in their social media feeds.  In a twist of innovation, the researchers are also promoting a new app called “Wait App,” which not only estimates how long you’ll have to wait for anything but also allows you to send virtual “waiting” emoji to your friends to express solidarity. After all, nothing says “I care” like a cute digital representation of an hourglass while your friend is stuck in traffic.  As we all know, the world is increasingly moving toward efficiency, yet somehow, we’ve managed to turn waiting into an Olympic sport. So the next time you’re stuck in a queue, remember: you’re not just wasting time; you’re participating in the future of existential thought. And who knows? Perhaps one day, we’ll turn this waiting game into an actual board game. Just don’t forget to roll the dice… whenever they arrive.

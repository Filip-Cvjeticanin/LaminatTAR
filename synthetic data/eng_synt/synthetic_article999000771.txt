In a groundbreaking revelation that has sent shockwaves through the scientific community, researchers at the Institute of Absurdity have discovered that the average human spends approximately 90% of their life waiting—mostly for Wi-Fi to reconnect and for their coffee to brew. This monumental study, titled “The Waiting Game: A Study of Human Existence,” was funded entirely by a group of serious procrastinators who insisted they would get around to it eventually.  The research team, led by Dr. Ima Punctuality, utilized cutting-edge technology and a sophisticated 1990s stopwatch to track the daily activities of over 100 volunteers. Participants were asked to record every moment they found themselves in a state of suspension, which varied from waiting at the DMV to contemplating how to respond to a friend’s cryptic text about a cat.  “People think they are busy,” explained Dr. Punctuality. “But in reality, they are just waiting… for everything. Waiting for inspiration, waiting for their laundry to finish, waiting for their phone to stop buzzing with notifications.” The study found that even while scrolling through social media, participants were primarily engaged in a complex form of waiting—waiting for likes, waiting for comments, and waiting for their friends to reveal the next great conspiracy theory about avocados.  The researchers also uncovered a startling trend: the average person spends about three years of their life waiting for someone to reply to a group chat. “We were shocked,” confessed assistant researcher, Kevin Quagmire. “That’s way more time than people spend actually making plans!”  In response to these findings, the Institute has begun issuing “Wait Less” kits, which include a stress ball, a pocket-sized motivational speaker (who encourages users to stop waiting and start doing), and a set of noise-canceling headphones to drown out the sounds of infinity. “It’s a revolutionary step toward reducing the national waiting time,” said Dr. Punctuality. “We envision a future where people can fill those endless minutes with activities like creating art, reading, or even just staring off into space in a productive manner.”  At the release party for the study, attendees reportedly spent most of the time waiting for the cake to be cut, leading to an impromptu discussion about whether cake really does taste better when earned through patience. As the clock ticked on, many wondered if they’d ever find out, forever trapped in the sorrowful loop of life’s greatest waiting rooms.

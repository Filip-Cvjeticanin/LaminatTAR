In a groundbreaking study released this week by the Institute of Overthinking and Unnecessary Worry (IOUW), researchers have concluded that the average adult spends approximately 27% of their waking hours contemplating existential crises that are unlikely to occur, while the remaining 73% is devoted to what’s for dinner. The study, aptly titled “Food or Fear: The Modern Dilemma,” highlights the significant mental gymnastics individuals perform daily, often culminating in a spiral of anxiety over whether they chose the correct avocado at the grocery store.  Dr. Mindy Noodle, the lead researcher, explained the findings: “We found that people will stand in front of an avocado display for an average of 14 minutes, debating the merits of each avocado’s ripeness, only to later question their life choices and consider whether they should have pursued a career in interpretive dance instead of accounting.” According to participants of the study, the morphing avocado debate often led to thoughts like, “What if the avocado is too ripe? Will I have to live with the shame of throwing away perfectly good guacamole?”  The research also found that individuals who engaged in yoga or meditation were no less likely to overthink their food choices. In fact, one participant revealed they spent 45 minutes in a yoga pose trying to find inner peace, only to later find themselves in a spiral of anxiety: “Do I really need to lose that extra five pounds, or can I just buy smaller pants?”   But it’s not just food that triggers the overthinking epidemic. Social media, with its endless feed of curated lives and overexposed brunches, was credited with ramping up the anxiety levels to unforeseen heights. “Scrolling through Instagram can lead to severe cases of the ‘FOMO Mix,’ a cocktail of jealousy and self-doubt that often results in poor dinner choices and spontaneous purchases of tie-dye sweatpants,” explained Dr. Noodle.  To combat this pervasive cycle of overthinking, the IOUW recommends a three-step program: Step one, make a meal plan. Step two, stick to it. Step three, forget about it and eat cereal for dinner. “Cereal has no existential implications,” said Dr. Noodle, chuckling. “Unless, of course, you start to question the meaning behind cornflakes, but that’s a whole different study.”   As the nation grapples with this new information, one question remains: will we ever truly escape the clutches of overthinking, or are we destined to weigh our life choices against the ripeness of our avocados forever? Only time—and a good bowl of cereal—will tell.

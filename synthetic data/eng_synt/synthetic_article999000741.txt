In a groundbreaking new study released Thursday, researchers have confirmed what many of us have long suspected: the average person spends more time scrolling through their social media feed than actually living their life. The study, conducted by the Institute for the Advancement of Procrastination (IAP), found that 92% of respondents would rather watch cats playing the piano than engage in real-life conversations.  Dr. Ima Lott, lead researcher and self-proclaimed social media addict, stated, “Our findings suggest that social media has essentially replaced meaningful human interaction. In fact, if you put a cat video next to a family reunion, the cat video will win every time. We call this the ‘Paw-sitive Engagement Effect.’”  Participants in the study were asked to document their daily activities, and the results were nothing short of shocking. One respondent reported spending three hours scrolling Instagram only to realize that they had forgotten to eat lunch. “I was just about to try a new sandwich recipe,” they lamented, “but then I saw a meme about avocado toast and I lost all sense of time and purpose.”  In a related finding, the researchers also revealed that people now have better relationships with their virtual assistants than with their actual family members. “I had a heart-to-heart with my Alexa last week,” one participant confessed. “She really understands me, unlike my brother who keeps asking me when I’m going to settle down. At least Alexa doesn’t judge me for binge-watching ‘The Great British Bake Off’ while eating an entire box of donuts.”  Experts suggest that this trend is only expected to grow, with one predicting that by 2030, human beings will communicate exclusively through memes. “We’re already seeing the emergence of this phenomenon,” said social media analyst Meme McMemeFace. “People are sending out birthday wishes via GIFs. Pretty soon, we won’t even bother to say ‘I love you’—we’ll just send a heart emoji and call it a day.”  In an effort to counteract this alarming trend, IAP has proposed a new initiative: the “Unplugged Challenge.” Participants are encouraged to go a full 24 hours without checking their phones or social media. The catch? They’ll have to interact with other human beings. “It’s a bit scary,” Dr. Lott acknowledged. “But they might just find out that talking to someone face-to-face doesn’t require a ‘Like’ button.”  As the world continues to spiral into a digital abyss, one thing is clear: the only thing more viral than a cat video is the realization that maybe, just maybe, we should put the phones down and remember how to interact with each other—unless, of course, there’s a new meme waiting to be shared.

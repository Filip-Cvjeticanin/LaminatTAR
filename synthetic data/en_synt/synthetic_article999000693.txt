In a groundbreaking move that has sent shockwaves through the scientific community, researchers at the Institute of Irrelevant Studies (IIS) have announced the discovery of a new continent, which they have aptly named "Distractionland." The researchers claim that this previously uncharted territory is located somewhere between “the back of your brain” and “the couch cushions of reality.”  The head researcher, Dr. Ima Nutter, stated during a press conference held in a coffee shop, "We always knew that people tend to lose focus, but we never imagined there was an entire landmass dedicated to it. Distractionland is a vast expanse filled with infinite scrolling, cat videos, and the haunting echoes of 'I’ll just watch one more episode.'"  The team of scientists, equipped with state-of-the-art snacking technology and a subscription to every streaming service known to humankind, embarked on a year-long expedition into the depths of their own procrastination. After weeks of what they described as “extensive research,” they reported that Distractionland is home to several unique species, including the Legendary Sofa Slouch, the Mythical Social Media Scroller, and the Elusive Snack Monster—also known as “That One Cookie You Swore You’d Save for Later.”  Dr. Nutter elaborated, "We were particularly surprised to find the rare breed of the Last-Minute Dash. This creature is often sighted as deadlines approach and is characterized by an impressive sprint towards productivity, typically fueled by a sheer panic and an empty coffee pot."  In an unprecedented stroke of marketing genius, the IIS plans to offer guided tours of Distractionland for only $29.99, which includes a complimentary set of wireless earbuds to drown out the world while you explore. “We want to provide a safe space for people to embrace their distractions,” Dr. Nutter explained. “You can come and lose track of time without judgment. Who knows, you might even find yourself! Or at least an old sandwich.”  Critics have raised concerns about the legitimacy of this discovery, citing that most people already spend significant time in Distractionland without a passport. In response, the IIS has launched an intercontinental “Stay Productive” campaign, with slogans like “Why Do Today What You Can Put Off Until Tomorrow?” and “If You Can’t Beat Procrastination, Join It.”  As for Dr. Nutter and his team, they plan to continue their research, with upcoming projects including “The Psychology of Netflix Bingeing” and “Understanding Why You’d Rather Clean the Bathroom Than Write That Essay.” Distractionland, it seems, is just the beginning of an exciting journey into the realms of human avoidance.
